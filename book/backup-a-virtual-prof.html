Ask the Virtual Prof

A chatbot that has read Chapters 1–6. It finds the passages that answer your question, shows you where to read them, and can explain them in its own words with a small open language model that runs on your device.

1Ask a question

The Virtual Prof answers from the book and nothing else. With AI explanations off, it replies instantly with the best passages, highlighted and linked. Turn them on and a language model reads those passages and writes a short answer that cites them; the model is downloaded once from the Materials & the Environment organisation on Hugging Face and then runs in your browser, with no server and no account.

2How it works

A language model small enough to run in a browser cannot remember a whole book, and when it tries it makes numbers up. The Virtual Prof therefore separates finding from explaining, a design known as retrieval-augmented generation.

Retrieval is lexical: it matches words, not meanings. It is fast, transparent and needs no download, and the synonym list covers the vocabulary of the book. You can watch it work below: type a question and see the passages it would hand to the model, with their scores.

3The models

Two open models from the Qwen3 family are offered, used in their non-thinking mode, converted to ONNX and quantised to 4 bits for Transformers.js. They are published under the MaterialsandtheEnvironment organisation on Hugging Face, with the book's index alongside them as a dataset; if a model is not found there, the page falls back to the public ONNX build it was copied from.

With WebGPU (recent Chrome or Edge), answers start within a second or two; on the CPU fallback, expect several seconds per sentence. The download is cached by the browser, so it happens once.

4Limits

  • It only knows the book. Questions outside Chapters 1–6 get an honest “not in the notes”, or a poor match. That is by design.
  • Small models still slip. A 0.6B model can misread a passage, cite the wrong note or refuse a question its notes do answer. Every claim carries a citation: check it.
  • Retrieval is literal. If a question uses words the book does not, rephrase it with the book's terms (modulus, embodied energy, eco-index, reinforcement form).
  • Figures are not read. The prof sees captions and text, not the interactive charts. For exact rankings, use the figure itself; the prof will tell you which one.

5From a small model to a virtual prof

An earlier version of this chapter fine-tuned a 360-million-parameter model (matenv-smollm2-360m) on 2 400 questions generated from nine calculators of Chapters 1–4. It learned to call the right calculator for about 80% of unseen questions, up from 0% for the base model, and its direct answers rose from 16% to 72% correct. It showed that a small model is a good router and a poor memory. The Virtual Prof keeps that lesson and scales it to the whole book: the memory is the book itself, searched exactly; the model only reads and explains. The training material remains in the repository's training folder.

Reproduce

python3 tools/build_prof_index.py rebuilds the knowledge base (data/prof_index.js and .json) from the chapters and their data files; run it after editing a chapter. python3 tools/publish_virtual_prof.py (or training/publish_virtual_prof.ipynb on Colab) copies the two ONNX models and the index to the Hugging Face organisation with model cards. The assistant itself is assets/js/prof.js; the floating button is assets/js/prof-widget.js.
