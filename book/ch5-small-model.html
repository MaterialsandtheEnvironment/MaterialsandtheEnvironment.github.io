Chapter 5 · Materials & the Environment

A Model That Read the Book

Turn the data and rules of Chapters 1–4 into thousands of questions, teach a free 360-million-parameter language model to answer them, and run it in your browser. Then measure what it learned.

1Why a small model, and why tools?

Large language models talk fluently about materials. Asked to score materials for a design, though, GPT-4 and its peers land about 3 points from expert answers on a 0–10 scale, rate almost everything too highly, and barely change their answer when the design brief changes. They have read about materials; they have not been taught to select them.

This chapter takes the opposite route. The model is tiny (SmolLM2-360M, about 270 MB once compressed, small enough to run inside a web page) and it is taught one narrow job: the questions that Chapters 1–4 can answer. It learns two ways of answering:

  • Direct: write the answer from memory, as a chatbot would.
  • With tools: translate the question into a call to one of the book's exact calculators and let the calculator answer. The model chooses what to compute; code does the computing.

The second way is the important one. A 360-million-parameter network cannot hold the stiffness of 22 materials and the stresses of 1 516 brackets reliably in its weights. It can learn to map a sentence to the right formula and arguments, and that skill generalises to phrasings it has never seen.

2The book as nine exact tools

Each tool is a few lines of code over data already used in the book, and each reproduces a number printed in an earlier chapter. Ranking the 22 materials for a light, stiff panel by E^{1/3}/\rho returns softwood pine, hardwood oak and plywood, as in Figure 1.1. The value function at \alpha_1 = £0.5/kg returns nodular cast iron with V = 0.98, as in Ashby's table. The TOSM tool finds 22 admissible runs at a safety factor of 1.25, with bracket 120 in titanium the lowest in carbon at 65.9 t, as in Chapter 4.

3Writing 2 400 questions

Nobody wants to write thousands of exam questions by hand, and nobody needs to: the tools know the answers. A generator picks a tool and random arguments (a loading case, a safety factor, a subset of materials), phrases the question with one of several templates and a few synonyms ("rigid shaft", "stiff beam in bending"; "nylon" for polyamides), and records two targets: the tool call and the exact answer sentence.

The test set is built so that memorising is not enough. A quarter of the templates for each tool are held out: the 240 test questions are worded in ways the model never sees during training.

4Fine-tuning with LoRA

Full fine-tuning would update all 362 million weights. Low-rank adaptation (LoRA) freezes them and learns, for each weight matrix W, a small correction made of two thin matrices:

W' = W + \frac{\alpha}{r}\, B A, \qquad B \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times r},\; A \in \mathbb{R}^{r\times k},\; r = 16 \ll d .

That is under 2% of the parameters, it fits a free Colab GPU, and after training BA is merged back into W, so the published model is an ordinary SmolLM2. Only the assistant's reply counts in the loss; the system prompt and question are context.

5Ask the model

Type a question or pick one from the held-out test set. The model is downloaded once from Hugging Face and runs on your device.

6What did it learn?

Accuracy on held-out questions, for the base model and the fine-tuned one. A tool-mode answer counts as correct when the JSON call has the right tool and arguments, which guarantees the right final answer. A direct answer counts as correct when it names the right material, family, bracket or number.

7Limits

  • Narrow by design. The model knows the nine tools and nothing else. Outside them it should answer none; a 360M model will sometimes force a question into the nearest tool instead.
  • Direct answers are recall. Without tools the model reproduces answers it saw during training and guesses the rest. Its numbers should not be trusted.
  • The tools inherit the book's assumptions. Teaching values for strengths and prices (Chapter 1), representative family properties (Chapter 3) and a re-used titanium geometry for every alloy (Chapter 4) all pass straight into the answers.
  • Templates are not people. The test questions are held-out templates, not questions written by students. A real evaluation would use questions from a class.

The broader lesson matches the one from the large models: language is a good interface to engineering knowledge and a poor store of it. A small model that knows when to call the right calculator is more trustworthy than a large one asked to remember.
